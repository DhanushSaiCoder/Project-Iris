Good — you built something useful, but you mixed automation and ambiguity and that’s why users are lost. I’ll be blunt: the flow is *too* clever for its own good. It surprises people, and surprise is the enemy of trust — especially for visually impaired users. Below is a ruthless, practical breakdown and a better replacement state machine you can implement *today*.

# 1) Primary usability flaws (what’s breaking)

1. **Automation Surprise** — switching from `guiding` → `recording` automatically without an explicit, obvious cue makes users unsure whether the app is doing something or failing. They don’t feel in control.
2. **Poorly signaled readiness** — “object in correct position and held stable” is abstract. Users don’t know when they’ve reached that threshold or why the app will record now.
3. **Timing ambiguity** — automatic countdown begins unexpectedly. Users can’t cancel easily or prepare to hold still.
4. **Insufficient multimodal feedback** — on-screen text alone is not enough for visually impaired users. Audio/haptic cues are underspecified and inconsistent.
5. **No visible stability metric** — users can’t see or hear how close they are to “stable enough.” So they guess, move, re-try.
6. **Single-button entry point hides options** — “Start Guidance” offers no choice between manual capture and auto-capture, so preferences are unknown.
7. **Failures are opaque** — if recording fails (movement, occlusion, low light) the user gets “Measurement failed” with no reason or next-step guidance.
8. **Cognitive load from mixed control model** — blending passive automation with active user action without signposting forces constant monitoring/guessing.

# 2) Balance assessment: which side is it on?

It **leans too far into automation** while failing to provide *predictable* automation. Consequence: users feel out of control and anxious — they will either micromanage (defeat automation) or mistrust the app and use it incorrectly. For visually impaired users, predictability > speed. Automation must be explainable and cancellable.

# 3) Better state machine(s) — pick one and implement

Two practical alternatives. Both add clarity and preserve efficiency. Choose A (user-controlled) if you want predictability. Choose B (hybrid) if you must capture faster and provide power users with automation.

---

## A — **User-triggered capture (default — conservative, accessible)**

Flow: `idle` → `guiding` → `ready` → `capture-request` (user presses) → `countdown` → `recording` → `saved` → `reviewing`

**Details / UI & audio:**

* **idle:** “Step 1 of 3. Calibrate at 1 meter. Press Start Guidance.” (TTS + short vibration on button focus)
* **guiding:** Real-time directional audio cues: “Move closer” / “Move farther” / “OK — hold.” Also a continuous *stability meter* spoken every 0.5–1s: “Stability: 40%.” Haptics: light pulse frequency increases as stability increases.
* **ready:** When object is in target range and stability > threshold, announce: “Ready to capture. Press and hold Capture to start, or say ‘capture’.” Give an obvious single tactile/clickable big control.
* **capture-request:** On button press or voice command, start a **3s visible & audible countdown**: “Recording in 3…2…1. Hold still.” Allow cancel (tactile tap, voice “cancel”) during countdown.
* **recording:** “Recording — hold still” with subtle continuous tone and a short vibration at start and end. If movement exceeds threshold, announce “Movement detected — recording paused” and allow user to re-hold.
* **saved/reviewing:** “Measurement saved. Press Redo or Continue.” TTS + short success vibration.

**Why:** gives the user explicit action and clear opportunity to cancel or stabilize. No surprises.

---

## B — **Hybrid (recommended default with opt-in auto mode)**

Flow: `idle` → `guiding` → `in-range-unsteady` → `pre-recording-countdown` (announced + cancellable) → `recording` → `saved` → `reviewing`

**Key differences:**

* **Toggle at start:** On Start Guidance, app prompts: “Auto-capture ON. Say ‘manual’ to switch.” Keep a setting for user preference.
* **Staged guidance:**

  * *in-range-unsteady:* “You’re close — a little closer and hold.” Stability % updates.
  * *pre-recording-countdown:* When stability crosses the threshold, **announce**: “Auto-capture will begin in 3 seconds. Say ‘now’ to start immediately, or ‘stop’ to cancel.” Then do a **3-second audible countdown** and a distinct haptic pattern.
* **Cancel options:** Voice (“stop”), two-finger tap, or big Cancel button during countdown.
* **Recording & feedback** as in A.
* **If user prefers speed:** reduce countdown to 1 second; otherwise use 3 seconds by default.

**Why:** preserves automation speed while removing surprise — the app tells the user exactly *when* it will record and how to stop it.

## State diagram (simple text)

```
idle
  └─(Start Guidance)→ guiding
                       ├─(in-range & stable?)→ ready/pre-recording
                       │                       └─(user-cancel?)→ guiding
                       └─(user presses Capture)→ countdown → recording → saved → reviewing
```

Implement clear events: `start_guidance`, `range_established`, `stability_crossed`, `capture_command`, `countdown_cancel`, `movement_detected`, `save_confirm`, `redo`.

# 4) Onboarding — what to say and how to practice

Users must *experience* the flow before relying on it.

**Short audio-first onboarding (30–45s)**

* 3-step voice-run demo:

  1. “This app needs three measurements at 1, 2 and 3 meters. I’ll guide you.”
  2. Do a **practice capture** with dummy data: guide → ready → announce countdown → record → saved. Let user hear the entire cycle.
  3. Quick tips: “If you want full control, say ‘manual’ at the start. Say ‘stop’ to cancel a countdown. Press and hold Capture to start immediately.”

**Practice mode:** Let user run a full practice and redo without datastore changes. Use exaggerated audio/haptic cues.

**Concise onboarding copy:** (TTS-friendly)

* “You will be guided and told when we’ll record. When you hear ‘Ready to capture,’ press Capture or say ‘capture’. During countdown say ‘stop’ to cancel. Try practice now.”

# 5) Accessibility-specific audio & haptic rules (must ship)

1. **Always speak the step and distance** at each state change: “Step 2: Calibrate at 2 meters.”
2. **Announce readiness explicitly**: “Ready to record in 3 seconds.” Never start a countdown silently.
3. **Provide stability feedback** every ≈0.5–1s: “Stability: 70% — almost there.”
4. **Distinct earcons**: beep pattern for “move closer”, rising tone for “ready”, two short tones for “start recording”. Keep them consistent.
5. **Haptic patterns**: low single pulse for guidance ticks; steady small buzz when stable; long buzz on save.
6. **Voice cancel commands**: support at least one word (“stop” or “cancel”) and a physical gesture (two-finger tap) to account for noisy environments.

# 6) Stability UX: visual + audio affordances

* **Stability meter** (0–100) visual and spoken: e.g., “Stability 84%.”
* **Target band indicator:** say “In-range” vs “Too close/Too far.”
* **Movement smoothing:** show smoothed stability (use short moving average) so the meter doesn’t jitter; jitter causes panic.
* **Threshold hysteresis:** require stability > T for S ms and remain > (T - hysteresis) to avoid flip-flop. Example: T=80%, S=600ms, hysteresis=10%.

# 7) Failure handling — be explicit

* If recording fails: speak reason: “Recording failed — too much movement. Try again or press Redo.”
* Timeouts: “No object detected for 10s. Try using a flat, high-contrast object.”
* Rejection: provide actionable tips: “Move object slightly left or right; remove background clutter.”

# 8) Settings & power-user options

* **Default conservative mode** for new users.
* **Advanced settings:** Auto-capture toggle, countdown length (0/1/2/3s), stability threshold slider. Save preference.
* **Voice & haptics volume** settings.

# 9) Quick dev checklist & acceptance criteria

* [ ] Announce state transitions via TTS.
* [ ] Provide stability meter and speak percentage.
* [ ] Show and speak “Ready to capture” before any automatic countdown.
* [ ] Allow cancel during countdown via voice and tap.
* [ ] Distinct tones for guidance/ready/start/success.
* [ ] Practice mode in onboarding.

* **KPIs** to validate: >90% successful captures in ≤20s per step during usability test; user-reported confusion <10% on first attempt; completion rate for full 3-step calibration ≥95%.

# 10) Suggested exact audio snippets (copy-ready)

* Idle: “Calibration. Step 1 of 3. Calibrate at one meter. Press Start Guidance when ready.”
* Guiding approaching: “Move closer.”
* Guiding receding: “Move farther.”
* Stability updates: “Stability sixty percent.”
* Ready (manual mode): “Ready to capture. Press Capture or say ‘capture’.”
* Pre-recording (auto): “Auto-capture in 3 seconds. Say ‘stop’ to cancel.”
* Recording start: “Recording — hold still.”
* Saved: “Measurement saved.”
* Failure: “Recording failed due to movement. Please hold still and try again.”

# Final blunt take

Your flow tried to be smart but forgot to *tell* users what it's doing. Fix that by making automation **visible, announced, and cancellable** — or hand control to the user. Don’t remove control in the name of speed. Predictability builds trust; trust makes the calibration usable.
